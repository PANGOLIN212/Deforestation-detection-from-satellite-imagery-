# Deforestation Detection from Satellite Imagery: A Kenyan Forest Case Study

Change detection on two-date Sentinel-2-style imagery: classic index rules versus a random forest on spectral, temporal and neighbourhood features, with cloud masking, honest threshold selection, patch-level and area evaluation, and a ranked alert list for field checks.

## Short Description

> A satellite change-detection pipeline for forest loss: NDVI/NBR baselines, cloud and shadow masking, a random forest on 39 spectral, temporal and context features evaluated leave-scene-out, minimum mapping unit, patch-level recall, area estimation, and error analysis by cause, built around Kenya's water-tower forests.

## Why Kenya

Kenya's five "water towers" (the Mau Forest Complex, the Aberdare Range, Mount Kenya, Mount Elgon and the Cherangani Hills) feed the rivers that supply cities, farms, tea estates and hydropower. They face pressure from illegal logging, charcoal burning, farmland expansion and settlement. Forest managers cannot patrol such large areas on foot, so satellite change detection is one of the few ways to see clearing soon after it happens.

## The Task, and Why It Is Hard

Given two images of the same place, label each pixel as **deforested** (forest at date 1, cleared by date 2). Most apparent change is **not** deforestation:

| Change that is NOT deforestation | Why it fools simple methods |
|----------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Seasonal drying of grass and crops | NDVI drops just as it does when forest is cleared |
| Crop harvest | Bare soil appears, just as after clearing |
| Clouds and cloud shadows | Large spectral change with no change on the ground |
| Selective logging (canopy thinning) | The forest is still there |
| Misregistration and sensor noise | Spurious change along every edge |

*The right-hand panel is the change in NDVI. Deforestation, harvest, drying and clouds all look alike there.*

## Data

> **Data note:** the scenes are **synthetic**, generated by `src/synthetic_scenes.py`. Reflectance signatures are typical illustrative values, and the scene names only echo Kenyan forest blocks. The results demonstrate the method, **not the state of any real forest**. The pipeline expects arrays shaped `(6, H, W)` per date, so real Sentinel-2 chips can be used instead (see "Using real data").

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Scenes | 8, each 192 × 192 pixels at 10 m (1.9 km square); 295,000 pixels in total |
| Bands | B2 blue, B3 green, B4 red, B8 near-infrared, B11 and B12 shortwave infrared |
| Dates | Two per scene, with different sun angle, season, calibration and a sub-pixel shift |
| True deforestation | 155 ha (8.0% of the forest) in 92 patches from 0.3 ha to 25 ha |
| Included nuisances | Harvest, seasonal drying, selective logging, clouds and shadows, misregistration |

![Spectral signatures](images/02_spectral_signatures.png)

## Approach

1. **Baselines:** thresholds on ΔNDVI, ΔNBR, and ΔNDVI with a forest mask and cloud mask, tuned on training scenes
2. **Cloud and shadow mask:** bright blue means cloud; uniform darkening of all bands between dates means shadow
3. **Random forest on 39 per-pixel features:** date 1 and date 2 bands and indices, differences, and neighbourhood context
4. **Leave-scene-out validation:** 4 folds, each testing on 2 scenes the model never saw
5. **Threshold chosen without peeking:** for each fold, the threshold comes from the other folds' predictions only
6. **Post-processing:** 3 × 3 opening and minimum mapping unit (MMU), evaluated rather than assumed
7. **Error analysis by cause**, patch-level recall by clearing size, and area estimates
8. **A ranked alert list** for field checks

## Results

### Why single-index rules are not enough

![NDVI change by cause](images/03_ndvi_change_by_cause.png)

| Method (pixel level, held-out scenes) | Precision | Recall | F1 | IoU |
|---------------------------------------|-----------|--------|-----|-----|
| ΔNDVI threshold | 0.269 | 0.916 | 0.416 | 0.263 |
| ΔNBR threshold | 0.525 | 0.871 | 0.655 | 0.487 |
| ΔNDVI + forest mask + cloud mask | 0.550 | 0.894 | 0.681 | 0.516 |
| **Random forest, all features, calibrated threshold** | **0.923** | **0.920** | **0.922** | **0.855** |

The rules find most clearings but flag far too much else. Splitting their false positives by cause (hectares):

| Cause of false alarm | Best rule | Random forest |
|----------------------|-----------|---------------|
| Cloud / shadow | 23.3 | 5.2 |
| Crop harvest | 54.4 | **0.0** |
| Seasonal drying | 5.2 | **0.0** |
| Selective logging | 17.3 | 3.7 |
| Forest noise / misregistration | 6.6 | 2.3 |

![Baseline false positives](images/04_baseline_false_positives.png)

### What each feature group adds (default threshold 0.5, cloud mask applied)

| Features | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|----------|-----------|--------|-----|
| Spectral change only | 0.709 | 0.967 | 0.818 |
| + date 1 and date 2 spectra (was it forest?) | 0.769 | 0.969 | 0.857 |
| + neighbourhood context | 0.796 | 0.974 | **0.876** |

Importance by group: change features 73%, date 1 spectra 10.5%, context 10.4%, date 2 spectra 6.2%.

### The threshold matters more than the post-processing

The forest is trained on a rebalanced sample (about 25% positives) while the real share is a few percent, so its probabilities are inflated and the default 0.5 is too low. **Choosing the threshold on the other folds' predictions** (0.80 to 0.85) lifts precision from 0.796 to 0.923 at almost the same recall, taking F1 from **0.876 to 0.922**. The threshold for a scene is never tuned on that scene.

Extra size filtering then stops helping, because it trades recall for precision:

| Minimum mapping unit | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|----------------------|-----------|--------|-----|
| None | 0.923 | 0.920 | **0.922** |
| 0.1 ha | 0.953 | 0.863 | 0.906 |
| 0.2 ha | 0.956 | 0.860 | 0.905 |
| 0.5 ha | 0.956 | 0.808 | 0.876 |

A monitoring team with few rangers to dispatch may still prefer a 0.2 ha MMU for fewer false alarms. That is a policy choice, and the F1 criterion alone favours no filtering.

![Precision recall](images/06_precision_recall.png)

### The cloud mask

Recall 81.3% and precision 77.0% on the truth masks, flagging 15.3% of all pixels as no-data. Applying it lifts F1 by about 0.02 for every feature set. The few cloud pixels it misses still account for 5.2 ha of the forest's false alarms.

![Cloud mask](images/05_cloud_mask.png)

### Small clearings are the hardest

![Error sources](images/08_error_sources.png)

| Clearing size | Patches | Detected |
|---------------|---------|----------|
| < 0.5 ha | 21 | 71.4% |
| 0.5 to 1.5 ha | 38 | 86.8% |
| 1.5 to 5 ha | 27 | 100% |
| > 5 ha | 6 | 100% |

Overall, 88.0% of the 92 patches are detected. **Small illegal clearings are the hardest to catch at 10 m resolution**, and they are often the early warning signal.

### Area estimates

The mapped total is 145 ha against 146 ha true (−0.3%), but per-scene errors range from −9.7% to +23.3%. The common correction `mapped × precision / recall` is **exact by arithmetic** when precision and recall come from the same data, so the notebook tests it honestly, adjusting each scene with the other scenes' precision and recall. It **does not help** here (mean absolute error 7.5% mapped against 8.8% adjusted for scenes over 10 ha), because precision and recall are almost equal and the correction factor is near 1. A defensible area estimate with confidence intervals needs a stratified sample of independent reference points.

![Area estimates](images/09_area_estimates.png)

### A ranked alert list

For the held-out scene aberdare_b, the raw map has 50 patches. Keeping only patches of at least 0.2 ha leaves 11 alerts covering 22.7 ha, **91% of which overlap real clearing**. These are ranked by size and model confidence for field checks.

### A note on the leakage experiment

A random-pixel split scored F1 0.926 against 0.923 for a scene-wise split, with the same model and sampling. **The gap is negligible here, so this experiment does not demonstrate a leakage problem.** All scenes come from the same simulator and are nearly interchangeable. Real imagery varies much more between places and dates, so scene- or region-wise validation remains the correct design, but this synthetic result is not evidence that it matters.

## Key Design Decisions

| Decision | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Leave-scene-out validation | Neighbouring pixels are near-copies. It measures performance on a new place |
| Threshold chosen from other folds | A threshold tuned on the data it is scored on is optimistic |
| Cloud mask applied, and its misses still scored | No-data is reported as no-data, but leftover cloud errors count |
| Pixels under cloud cannot be called deforested | You cannot verify what you cannot see |
| Selective logging labelled "not deforestation" | The forest is still there |
| MMU chosen by evaluation | It is a precision and recall trade-off, not a free improvement |
| Area adjustment tested leave-one-scene-out | Using the same data would be circular |

## Using Real Data

1. **Get imagery.** Sentinel-2 L2A surface reflectance is free from the Copernicus Data Space or Google Earth Engine. `real_data/gee_export_sentinel2.js` exports two seasonal composites for an area (**untested**: I could not run Earth Engine while building this, so check it carefully and confirm collection and band names). Use the **same season in both years**
2. **Load the GeoTIFFs** as arrays of shape `(6, H, W)` (band order B2, B3, B4, B8, B11, B12), for example with `rasterio` (`f.read()`; also untested here)
3. **Labels.** Global tree-cover-loss maps (such as the Hansen Global Forest Change data) can provide weak labels, but they record *tree cover loss*, which **includes harvest of plantations**. Kenya has many cypress, pine and eucalyptus plantations on harvest cycles, so for real deforestation you need a plantation mask, local knowledge from the Kenya Forest Service, or manual interpretation of sample areas
4. Replace the scene list in the notebook, keep the leave-scene-out folds (use separate regions), and expect lower scores than here

## Limitations

- **Synthetic data.** Real scenes have far more variety: terrain shadows, haze, fire scars, rivers, messy cloud edges and plantation cycles
- **Resolution.** At 10 m, clearings below about 0.2 ha are hard to see. Higher-resolution imagery or Sentinel-1 radar (which sees through cloud) would help
- **Two dates only.** Dense time series handle clouds, seasonality and regrowth much better
- **Reference labels** from real products and interpreters have errors of their own
- **Small test set:** 8 scenes and 92 patches, so per-scene and per-size estimates are uncertain

## Responsible Use

- Alerts should be **verified by people** (rangers, local forest officers) before any enforcement action
- Flagging a clearing is not identifying who did it, and communities living near forests can be affected by enforcement. Involve them, and use the data to support restoration and livelihoods, not only penalties
- Satellite monitoring should complement local knowledge, not replace it

## Possible Improvements

- Use a convolutional neural network or U-Net (not included, because deep-learning libraries could not be tested in the build environment)
- Add Sentinel-1 radar to see through cloud
- Use dense time series with break detection instead of two dates
- Add a plantation mask and a regrowth class
- Estimate area with a design-based reference sample and confidence intervals
